\begin{doublespacing}
Accurate estimation of the State of Health (SOH) and Remaining Useful Life (RUL) of lithium-ion batteries is a prerequisite for the safe and economical operation of Electric Vehicles (EVs). Prognostics research currently divides between model-based methods, which are interpretable but computationally demanding, and data-driven methods, which are accurate but physically unconstrained and difficult to deploy on the resource-limited microcontrollers of a Battery Management System (BMS).

This thesis develops and evaluates a hybrid physics-informed framework that regularizes a lightweight Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) with tractable empirical degradation physics. Instead of solving electrochemical Partial Differential Equations (PDEs), the framework embeds two Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) priors --- the Verhulst logistic law and a double-exponential law --- in the loss function, in two variants that are compared head-to-head: a fixed closed-form prior (route~A) and a learnable ODE residual whose parameters train jointly with the network (route~B). Ten models --- two non-neural floors (a double-exponential curve fit and a support vector regressor), LSTM, GRU, two Transformer scales, a Neural-ODE, a matched-capacity no-physics control, and the two hybrids --- are evaluated on the NASA PCoE and CALCE datasets under a leak-free leave-one-cell-out protocol in which every learned model receives the same fixed-scale cycle input, together with standard PHM prognostic metrics, a physics-design ablation with a de-confound control, and a Cortex-M4 deployment profile.

The learnable route-B hybrid attains the lowest mean SOH RMSE in the benchmark ($0.0292 \pm 0.0100$) and the highest physical consistency of any learned model (monotone fraction $0.884$). A matched-capacity control --- the identical 41k-parameter backbone and time input with the physics loss switched off --- scores $0.0377$, attributing the gain of more than a fifth to the physics residual rather than to capacity (the improvement holds on 11 of 12 paired runs). The hybrid's accuracy advantage over a thirteen-times-larger high-capacity Transformer ($-21.0\%$ mean RMSE, reflecting the Transformer's much larger run-to-run variance) is not statistically significant with four cells, while its physical-consistency advantage holds on every run; both are stated descriptively. The fixed prior (route~A, $0.0382$) trails the learnable variant on every cell, and an evaluated homoscedastic uncertainty weighting shows no reliable benefit over a fixed loss weight; the negative results are reported. Quantized to int8, an 11k-parameter deployment variant measures 20.2~KB of flash and 1.25~KB of RAM --- about 2\% of a Cortex-M4 budget and thirty times less flash than the high-capacity Transformer --- trading accuracy ($0.0469 \pm 0.0217$) for footprint while retaining the highest physical consistency of the deployment candidates. The framework's main limitation is also identified and explained: on a zero-shot transfer to the much longer-lived CALCE cell, the learnable variant degrades because its cycle coordinate extrapolates beyond the training range, a failure mode with a diagnosed mechanism and candidate mitigations. The thesis concludes that a learnable physics residual improves a small recurrent network where added capacity does not, yielding a physically plausible and deployable approach to on-board battery prognostics at realistic data scales.
\end{doublespacing}
