\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis addressed the industrial need for battery prognostic tools that are
accurate, interpretable, and computationally feasible for deployment on edge
devices within Battery Management Systems (BMS). The literature review established
that existing approaches force a compromise between the accuracy of ``black-box''
deep learning and the interpretability of ``white-box'' physical models, and that
most physics-informed work in the battery domain relies on computationally
expensive PDE-PINNs.

To bridge this gap, the work proposed a \textbf{lightweight hybrid framework} that
regularizes a data-driven Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) with tractable empirical
degradation physics. Two physics mechanisms were implemented and \emph{compared}: a
fixed closed-form prior used as a soft penalty (\textbf{route~A}, using Verhulst
and double-exponential laws) and a \emph{learnable} ODE-residual whose parameters
are trained jointly with the network (\textbf{route~B}). The framework was
evaluated on the full NASA leave-one-cell-out protocol (four folds, three
seeds), a zero-shot cross-dataset transfer to CALCE, a physics-design ablation
with a de-confound control, data-scarcity and overfitting stress tests against
two Transformer scales, and a Cortex-M4 deployment-footprint analysis.

The measured results support the following conclusions, which are more precise
than the interim report's claim that the hybrid always wins:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Learnable physics (route~B).} Under a leak-free,
    order-independent protocol in which every learned model receives the same
    fixed-scale cycle input, the learnable ODE-residual attains the lowest mean
    SOH RMSE in the benchmark ($0.0295 \pm 0.0096$) and the highest physical
    consistency of any learned model ($0.902$). A matched-capacity control ---
    the identical 41k backbone and time input with the physics loss switched
    off --- scores $0.0373$, so the gain ($-21\%$) is attributable to the
    physics residual rather than to capacity or to the time input. Route~B is
    better than or equal to the fixed prior (route~A, $0.0364$) on every cell,
    with the largest margin on the atypical B0006, which begins above rated
    capacity with heavy regeneration.
    \item \textbf{Transformer comparison.} The thesis started from the position
    that attention models lead on accuracy but are too heavy for standard BMS
    hardware. The measurements support that position in a specific form: at this
    data scale, scaling the Transformer to a high-capacity size (225k
    parameters, 13$\times$ the edge-sized variant) buys no reliable accuracy
    return ($0.0372$ vs.\ $0.0395$, overlapping intervals) while costing
    $8.7\times$ the int8 flash. The hybrid's mean accuracy advantage over the
    heavy Transformer ($-20.7\%$, 8 of 12 runs) is not statistically
    significant; its physical-consistency advantage holds on all 12 runs and
    all 4 cells --- both stated descriptively, since four independent cells
    cannot support formal significance.
    \item \textbf{Baselines.} The purely data-driven baselines cluster at
    $0.037$--$0.042$ (heavy Transformer $0.0372$, LSTM $0.0381$, light
    Transformer $0.0395$, GRU $0.0400$), all behind route~B's $0.0295$; the
    LSTM fails on the atypical cell B0006 (per-cell RMSE $0.064$) exactly where
    a fleet BMS is most exposed, while B0005 is difficult for every model. The
    recurrent baselines lead the single-cell cross-dataset transfer (LSTM
    $0.0867$); route~B degrades out of distribution ($0.2094$) because its
    cycle coordinate extrapolates far outside the training range on the
    long-lived CALCE cell. This negative result has an identified mechanism and
    candidate fixes (coordinate saturation, or the zeroed-coordinate deployment
    path). Under the corrected post-EOL-masked prognostic protocol, the
    non-neural floors calibrate best overall; among deep models route~B leads
    $\alpha$-$\lambda$ ($0.38$) and the LSTM leads CRA ($0.35$).
    \item \textbf{Interpretability.} The physics priors were fit to per-cell
    data (the double-exponential beats the logistic by AIC on every cell), and
    route~B learns its own Verhulst parameters ($r\approx-0.89$, $K\approx0.87$
    in the fixed-scale cycle coordinate, taken from the best-validation
    checkpoint) during training, so the degradation law inside the model has
    physically meaningful, inspectable parameters.
    \item \textbf{Deployment footprint.} A matched-size route-B hybrid (hidden
    $=32$, $\approx$11k parameters) measures 20.2~KB of int8 flash (PyTorch
    dynamic quantization, qnnpack) with 1.25~KB of activation RAM --- 2\% of the
    Cortex-M4 flash budget, $30\times$ less flash and $11\times$ lower latency
    than the heavy Transformer, and $3.5\times$ less flash than the light one.
    The accuracy cost of shrinking from 41k to 11k parameters is stated directly
    ($0.0295 \rightarrow 0.0427 \pm 0.0213$, with high fold-to-fold variance):
    the deployed model is statistically indistinguishable from the Transformers
    on accuracy and retains the highest physical consistency of the deployment
    candidates ($0.889$); its case is plausibility per kilobyte, while peak
    accuracy belongs to the benchmark-size model.
    \item \textbf{Negative results.} The homoscedastic uncertainty weighting is
    no better than a well-chosen fixed weight, so the fixed weight is adopted
    for the main benchmark models; the reduced-protocol ablations proved
    noise-dominated and their design conclusions are superseded by the
    full-protocol matched-capacity control; and the deployed-size accuracy
    trade-off and route~B's cross-dataset failure are reported with their
    mechanisms. Reporting these outcomes explicitly defines the limits of the
    claims made.
\end{itemize}

In summary, the thesis shows that a learnable physics residual improves a
recurrent backbone where added capacity --- recurrent or attention --- does
not: at matched capacity the physics term reduces error by about a fifth and
produces the most physically plausible learned predictions, and the deployed
variant fits in 2\% of a Cortex-M4's flash. The framework's main identified
weaknesses, long-horizon extrapolation of the cycle coordinate across datasets
and the accuracy cost at the smallest deployment size, are explained and
bounded, with clear mitigations.

\section{Future Work}

The framework is reproducible end-to-end. Several directions could extend this
work:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic relaxation of the physics penalty.} The fixed-prior
    route~A degraded atypical cells. A schedule that relaxes the ODE-penalty weight
    in early epochs (letting the network establish empirical convergence before the
    physical constraint tightens) could recover route~A's accuracy on typical cells
    without its failure mode on atypical ones.
    \item \textbf{On-board deployment validation.} The int8 sizes are measured
    from serialized quantized models, but the Cortex-M4 latency remains an
    analytic MACs/clock estimate. Flashing the quantized export (ONNX or
    TFLite-Micro) to a physical M4 board and measuring wall-clock inference and
    energy per window would close the deployment loop.
    \item \textbf{Broader chemistries and operating conditions.} Both datasets are
    LCO; validating on LFP/NMC cells and on field-collected partial-cycle data would
    test the framework beyond the cross-load/cross-form-factor transfer studied
    here.
    \item \textbf{Coordinate saturation for long-horizon transfer.} Route~B's
    cross-dataset degradation traces to its cycle coordinate leaving the training
    range on trajectories much longer than the training cells. Saturating the
    coordinate at the training maximum, rescaling it per deployment fleet, or
    falling back to the zeroed-coordinate inference path for out-of-range cells
    are simple candidate fixes worth evaluating.
    \item \textbf{More cells for statistical power.} With four NASA cells the
    hybrid's mean accuracy edge over the heavy Transformer ($-20.7\%$, 8/12
    runs) cannot reach formal significance; evaluating on larger fleets
    (e.g.\ the Toyota/MIT dataset) would settle the accuracy question and
    re-test the uncertainty weighting where it is expected to matter.
    \item \textbf{Broader PIML baselines.} The present study compares against one
    physics-informed alternative (a continuous-time Neural-ODE). Extending the
    comparison to further PIML families under the same hardware constraint ---
    e.g.\ a closed-form-solution PINN head, a physics-guided feature model, or a
    reduced-order electrochemical surrogate --- would strengthen the positioning
    of the proposed hybrid within the PIML design space.
    \item \textbf{Per-cell online adaptation.} Because route~B learns its ODE
    parameters, a lightweight online update of those parameters during deployment
    could let a single shipped model specialize to the cell it is actually
    monitoring.
\end{itemize}
