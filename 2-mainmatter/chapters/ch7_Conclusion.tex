\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}

This thesis addressed the industrial need for battery prognostic tools that are
accurate, interpretable, and computationally feasible for deployment on edge
devices within Battery Management Systems (BMS). The literature review established
that existing approaches force a compromise between the accuracy of ``black-box''
deep learning and the interpretability of ``white-box'' physical models, and that
most physics-informed work in the battery domain relies on computationally
expensive PDE-PINNs.

To bridge this gap, the work proposed a \textbf{lightweight hybrid framework} that
regularises a data-driven Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) with tractable empirical
degradation physics. Two physics mechanisms were implemented and \emph{compared}: a
fixed closed-form prior used as a soft penalty (\textbf{route~A}, using Verhulst
and double-exponential laws) and a \emph{learnable} ODE-residual whose parameters
are trained jointly with the network (\textbf{route~B}). The framework was
evaluated on the full NASA leave-one-cell-out protocol (four folds, three
seeds), a zero-shot cross-dataset transfer to CALCE, a physics-design ablation
with a de-confound control, data-scarcity and overfitting stress tests against
two Transformer scales, and a Cortex-M4 deployment-footprint analysis.

The measured results support the following conclusions, which are more precise
than the interim report's claim that the hybrid always wins:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Learnable physics (route~B).} The learnable ODE-residual
    performed best overall: it attains the lowest mean SOH RMSE of all
    benchmarked models ($0.0307$), consistent accuracy on every unseen cell
    (per-cell RMSE $0.025$--$0.037$), and the highest physical consistency of any
    learned model ($0.939$), significantly higher than the SOTA-scale Transformer
    on all 12 folds ($p\approx5\times10^{-4}$). Its mean SOH RMSE is $28.3\%$
    below that Transformer in-distribution, although with only four cells the
    accuracy gap is not statistically significant ($p=0.092$). The fixed
    closed-form prior (route~A), in contrast, helps typical cells but degrades
    atypical ones: a prior fit on other cells cannot describe a cell such as
    B0006, which begins above rated capacity with heavy regeneration.
    \item \textbf{Transformer comparison.} The thesis started from the position
    that attention models lead on accuracy but are too heavy for standard BMS
    hardware. The measurements support a stronger claim: under the data scarcity
    typical of this application, scaling the Transformer to SOTA size (225k
    parameters, 13$\times$ the edge-sized variant) made it less accurate, a
    result the scarcity and generalisation-gap stress tests attribute to
    overfitting, while the physics-regularised hybrid's accuracy was stable from
    10\% to 100\% of the training data. At this data scale the physics prior
    contributes more than the additional attention capacity does.
    \item \textbf{Baselines.} The LSTM is the strongest purely data-driven
    baseline on unseen NASA cells (RMSE $0.0331$, statistically tied with
    route~B) but shares route~A's weakness on atypical cells, and the light
    Transformer leads the single-cell cross-dataset transfer. The route-B hybrid
    offers the best overall combination of accuracy, physical plausibility,
    prognostic calibration (best learned CRA, $0.30$), cross-dataset robustness
    and footprint, which is the balance a safety-critical BMS requires.
    \item \textbf{Interpretability.} The physics priors were fit to per-cell
    data (the double-exponential beats the logistic by AIC on every cell), and
    route~B learns its own Verhulst parameters ($r\approx-0.80$, $K\approx0.93$)
    during training, so the degradation law inside the model has physically
    meaningful, inspectable parameters.
    \item \textbf{Deployment footprint.} A matched-size route-B hybrid (hidden
    $=32$, $\approx$11k parameters) comes to 11.7~KB of int8 flash ($4\times$
    post-training-quantisation estimate of the measured fp32 size) with 1.25~KB
    of measured activation RAM: $1/20$ the flash and $1/11$ the latency of the
    SOTA-scale Transformer, while being more accurate ($0.0305$ vs.\ $0.0428$)
    and retaining the full-size model's leave-one-cell-out accuracy. It uses
    1.1\% of the Cortex-M4 flash budget ($<$256~KB RAM, $<$1~MB flash).
    \item \textbf{Negative results.} The homoscedastic uncertainty weighting did
    not improve on a well-chosen fixed weight on this dataset, so the fixed
    weight is adopted for the headline models. The de-confound ablation likewise
    attributes much of route~B's raw accuracy to its cycle-coordinate input,
    with the ODE residual contributing the statistically significant physical
    plausibility. The automatic weighting may still prove useful on larger,
    noisier datasets.
\end{itemize}

In summary, the thesis shows that a lightweight recurrent model with a learnable
physics regulariser is a practical route to deployable EV battery prognostics:
it is accurate, physically plausible, stable on unseen cells and at reduced data
volumes, and small enough for on-board inference.

\section{Future Work}

The framework is reproducible end-to-end. Several directions could extend this
work:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic relaxation of the physics penalty.} The fixed-prior
    route~A degraded atypical cells. A schedule that relaxes the ODE-penalty weight
    in early epochs (letting the network establish empirical convergence before the
    physical constraint tightens) could recover route~A's accuracy on typical cells
    without its failure mode on atypical ones.
    \item \textbf{On-board deployment validation.} The int8 sizes are $4\times$
    quantisation estimates and the Cortex-M4 latency is an analytic MACs/clock
    estimate. Producing a genuinely quantised export (ONNX or TFLite-Micro),
    flashing it to a physical M4 board, and measuring wall-clock inference and
    energy per window would close the deployment loop.
    \item \textbf{Broader chemistries and operating conditions.} Both datasets are
    LCO; validating on LFP/NMC cells and on field-collected partial-cycle data would
    test the framework beyond the cross-load/cross-form-factor transfer studied
    here.
    \item \textbf{More cells for statistical power.} With four NASA cells the
    hybrid's large mean accuracy edge over the SOTA Transformer ($-28.3\%$) falls
    short of significance; evaluating on larger fleets (e.g.\ the Toyota/MIT
    dataset) would settle the accuracy question and re-test the uncertainty
    weighting where it is expected to matter.
    \item \textbf{Per-cell online adaptation.} Because route~B learns its ODE
    parameters, a lightweight online update of those parameters during deployment
    could let a single shipped model specialise to the cell it is actually
    monitoring.
\end{itemize}
